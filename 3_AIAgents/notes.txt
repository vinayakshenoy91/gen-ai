- AI agents are goal driven rather than static rule driven. Meaning, given the actual goal of what 
we need to achieve, given a business process, 
the AI Agents could perceive, plan, reason, and act.

- This iterative self-learning loop of Perceive ➤ Plan ➤ Reason ➤ Act ➤ Reflect is made all possible 
with the help of language understanding models that can not only understand language but also reason, 
which is one of the critical aspects of the human brain—to reason, act, and reflect. “Reflection” is 
often the agent reviewing its own output against the original goal before finalizing an action. 
This continuous loop is what makes the AI agent autonomous.

- AI agents should be perceived as a progression toward autonomous rather than defining 
it as always autonomous.

Deterministic process: 
----------------------
- Rule-based logic and deterministic outcomes deliver efficiency, reliability, and control.
- A deterministic process may not be flexible or “intelligent,” but it’s consistent, dependable, 
and manageable.

- we’re now building ones that can chase goals on their own and adjust as the world around them changes. 

Embracing Uncertainty As a Design Principle
---------------------------------------------
- With autonomy, it’s the other way around—uncertainty is expected. These systems have to keep working 
even when the picture is incomplete or when surprises come up.That kind of behavior requires 
goal awareness, reasoning, and adaptability.

- Autonomous systems, on the other hand, are dynamic, and they watch what’s happening, measure their progress, 
and adjust their actions along the way. 

- engineers define boundaries, guardrails, and incentives that guide the system’s behavior.

- The role of the engineer shifts from hardcoding every instruction 
to designing intent, adaptability, and safe ways for the system


Ultimately, the mindset of autonomy prioritizes:
-------------------------------------------------
- Robustness over rigidity
- Situational awareness over predefined control
- Goal-directed agency over task repetition


The Agentic Leap: 

Reasoning – Drawing inferences from facts, chaining logic across multiple steps, 
and making decisions under uncertainty

Memory – Retaining relevant facts, previous context, or user preferences 
across interactions

Planning – Breaking goals into actionable steps, sequencing actions, and 
adapting when conditions change
